01 — The situation

A good restaurant that nobody can find

Guests who discover Benjamin's rate it 5.0 and come back. The problems are visibility, under-used capacity and missing systems — exactly what a Restaurant Manager fixes without major investment.

Today

  • No managed Google profile, website, social channels or delivery presence
  • Open to the public — but barely anyone knows
  • Closed Sundays; service ends 20:00; events and private dining under-sold
  • No structured cost, labour or guest data informing decisions

The opportunity

  • Affluent, older HA7 catchment with strong Jewish and Indian/Gujarati communities — family-occasion diners on our doorstep
  • A calm, service-led position no local competitor occupies
  • The restaurant is also Elysian's most visible sales asset for prospective buyers
  • Post-merger, Benjamin's can become the F&B reference model for 30+ group villages

03 — The plan

Five pillars, one principle: residents first

Pillar 1Visibility

Claim and complete the free digital basics (Google profile, listings, photography, social), activate the doorstep (70 adjacent apartments, Sunday lunch, afternoon tea), then earn local standing through clubs, societies and one ticketed event a month. Review target: from ~15 to 100+ at ≥4.5.

Pillar 2Operational efficiency

Daypart audit, then hours and rotas reshaped to measured demand. Menu engineering by margin and popularity; portion specs, waste log, supplier reviews. Labour and food cost tracked weekly within agreed bands.

Pillar 3Service & food quality

Written service standards for every step of the guest journey — trained, audited, never rushed. Consent-based resident preference records. Photographed plate specs, line checks, quarterly menu refresh shaped by the resident dining committee and nutrition-aware design.

Pillar 4Training & team

Daily briefings, cross-training across restaurant, bar and events; accredited development (food safety, allergens, WSET). Career pathways across the enlarged Elysian–Audley group — join to grow, stay to lead.

Pillar 5Data-driven decisions

Full value from systems the group already owns — Cubigo, Apicbase, Monika, Square — feeding one tool-agnostic weekly dashboard. Every pilot gets success criteria before launch and a stop/scale decision on evidence.

05 — The financial spine

Pilots earn their place, or they stop

All figures are stated planning assumptions, replaced with actuals by day 30. The model — not the placeholder numbers — is the commitment.

Sunday lunch — worked break-even

Incremental labour (2 kitchen, 2–3 FOH, ~30–35 hrs)~£500–600
Energy, consumables, marketing share~£100–150
Incremental Sunday cost~£650–750
Gross profit per cover (£30 × ~67% GM)~£20
Break-even~33–38 covers
12-month ambition: +25–40% covers and +10% spend per head ≈ £2,000–3,500 additional weekly revenue at validated margins.

Pre-agreed success gates (weeks 5–8 avg)

  • ≥ 45 covers per Sunday
  • ≥ £28 spend per head
  • Labour ≤ 45% of Sunday revenue during the pilot, with a route to ≤ 35% once established
  • ≥ 90% resident satisfaction — no decline vs weekday baseline
  • ≥ 20% repeat or resident-family bookings by week 8
All gates met → permanent. Covers trending up ≥10% week-on-week → extend four weeks. Otherwise → stop, publish the learning, redeploy. No pilot becomes permanent by default.

07 — Guardrails & asks

Hard rules, modest requirements

Resident protection — hard rules

  1. Priority booking: residents book any time; external windows open later and never displace a resident
  2. Protected capacity: resident tables every service; resident-only periods where demand warrants
  3. External cap per service, aligned to the 20–25% overall share target
  4. Concierge arrival process for external guests — security and tone preserved
  5. Communication before change: every pilot preceded by resident communication and committee consultation

What I need from management

  1. Reporting access to Cubigo, Apicbase, Monika and Square from day one
  2. Listings authority (Google, OpenTable, Tripadvisor) and a dining page on the Landsby web presence
  3. Modest launch budget — photography, print, signage
  4. Sign-off for the 8-week Sunday pilot against the gates in §05
  5. Training budget per head; co-promotion via Elysian channels
  6. Weekly GM check-in · monthly P&L · quarterly senior review — the same dashboard at every level
