# Benjamin's at The Landsby — Restaurant Manager Plan

**A 12-month plan to make Benjamin's the social heart of The Landsby and a known neighbourhood destination in Stanmore**

Prepared for: Senior Management, The Landsby / Elysian Residences (Audley Group)
Prepared by: Vasile Bogdan Godja — candidate, Restaurant Manager
Date: June 2026

---

## Executive summary

Benjamin's is a high-quality restaurant trading well below its potential. It sits inside a near-fully-occupied luxury later-living community of ~101 apartments, with an adjacent block of 70 private apartments next door, in one of outer London's most affluent suburbs — yet it is closed on Sundays, closes at 20:00, has almost no digital footprint, and holds only a handful of online reviews while nearby competitors hold hundreds.

This plan proposes a phased, low-capital programme built on five pillars:

1. **Visibility** — fix the digital basics, then build local awareness deliberately, starting with the audiences already on our doorstep.
2. **Operational efficiency** — match hours, rotas and menus to actual demand; manage food and labour cost with discipline, not cuts.
3. **Service and food quality** — codify standards so excellent service is a system, not a coincidence.
4. **Training and team development** — invest in people so quality survives busy days and staff turnover; create career paths within the enlarged Elysian–Audley group.
5. **Data-driven decision making** — full value from the systems the group already runs (Cubigo, Apicbase, Monika, Square), plus in-house-built automation, so every decision above is tested against numbers, not opinions.

The guiding principle throughout: **residents first**. Every external-revenue initiative is designed to enhance, never dilute, the resident experience — because the restaurant is also Elysian's most visible sales asset when prospective buyers visit.

The plan is deliberately phased — a concrete first 90 days (14/30/60/90), then build (months 4–6) and grow (months 7–12) — with a financial model and pre-agreed success gates on every pilot, so management can see progress and stop or scale any initiative on evidence. It asks for almost no capital: data access, a modest launch budget, authority to run time-boxed pilots, and a regular review rhythm.

## 2. Who we serve — local reality, not generic strategy

The plan is shaped around three concrete audiences, in priority order:

**1. Landsby residents (the foundation).** Older, affluent, independent; many live alone. For them the restaurant is not a treat — it should be an extension of home: somewhere to eat well daily, meet neighbours, and host family. Frequency, familiarity and social connection matter more than novelty. Nutrition-aware comfort food, generous service time, and hosted social formats are the levers.

**2. The immediate neighbourhood (the nearest growth).** Stanmore/HA7 skews older, owner-occupied and affluent. Two communities matter specifically:
- A large, long-established **Jewish community** — for whom Sunday (not Saturday) is the family dining occasion, and for whom familiar dishes (the matzo ball soup already on our menu is no accident) and multi-generational tables matter. We will not claim kosher certification — that is a different operation — but we can be deliberately welcoming: clear menu labelling, fish and vegetarian depth, Sunday family lunch.
- A significant **Indian and Gujarati Hindu community** — our existing Indian-influenced menu is an asset; a genuinely strong vegetarian offer (not one token dish) is the credibility test.
- Plus the **70 private apartments next door** — the nearest low-friction audience, currently unaddressed.

**3. Occasion-driven diners from the wider catchment.** Families seeking calm, quality Sunday lunch and celebration venues, who currently default to busy Turkish, Indian and gastropub venues in Edgware, Mill Hill and Hatch End. We do not compete with them on volume or price — we compete on setting, calm, parking, service and ease. "The calmer, service-led alternative" is an open position in this market.

### The proposition, in one line

The current menu reads broad — modern European, Indian-influenced, British favourites, rotating specials. The plan sharpens it to a single, ownable identity: **club-style British/European comfort dining, done properly, with carefully chosen Indian and Asian specials** — built around three hero offers:

1. **Weekday resident dining** — the everyday foundation: familiar, nutrition-aware, never rushed.
2. **Afternoon tea** — the signature occasion that suits the room, the demographic, and a daypart we already staff.
3. **Sunday family lunch** — the structural gap, aimed precisely at local family habits.

Everything else on the calendar (events, private dining, specials) hangs off this spine rather than competing with it.

## 3. The five pillars

### Pillar 1 — Visibility: from hidden gem to known name

**Phase A. Fix the basics (month 1, near-zero cost):**
- Claim and complete the Google Business Profile — hours, photos, menu, booking link, "open to the public" stated plainly. For a local restaurant this single free channel outweighs everything else.
- Standardise listings (OpenTable, Tripadvisor, SquareMeal): consistent hours, professional photography, clear messaging that non-residents are welcome.
- A simple one-page web presence and an Instagram/Facebook account with a realistic cadence (2–3 posts weekly: dishes, events, the terrace, the team).
- Build review volume ethically: a card/QR at bill presentation, staff prompted to invite happy guests to share. Target: from ~15 total reviews to 100+ within 12 months.

**Phase B. Activate the doorstep (months 2–4):**
- Personal letter and opening offer to the 70 adjacent private apartments.
- Re-launch **Sunday lunch** as a pilot (12:00–16:00, focused roast + sharing menu) — the single biggest structural gap, aimed precisely at local family habits. Run 8 weeks against pre-agreed success gates (defined below, §5) — keep, adjust or stop on the numbers, not on sentiment.
- Push **afternoon tea** as the second signature occasion — it suits the room, the demographic and the daypart we already staff.

**Phase C. Earn local standing (months 4–12):**
- Host local clubs and societies (bridge, golf, U3A, WI, synagogue and temple social groups) for fixed-menu lunches — midweek volume at predictable cost.
- One ticketed event per month (wine dinner, supplier evening, seasonal celebration), co-promoted with The Landsby's existing lifestyle programme so sales and lifestyle teams benefit from the same calendar.
- Local press and community publications: the story ("a restaurant inside Stanmore's most striking building — and you're welcome in it") is genuinely newsworthy locally.

*What we will not do:* discounting platforms, external delivery apps, or anything that floods the room and degrades the resident experience. The only off-premise activity in year one is **"Landsby at Home"** — in-building delivery to residents' apartments, which carries no brand risk and a clear wellbeing benefit. External delivery is explicitly out of scope until dining-room economics are proven and management decides otherwise.

### Pillar 2 — Operational efficiency: capacity matched to demand

- **Daypart audit (month 1):** map covers and revenue by hour/day from POS and bookings. Decide on evidence where the quiet hours genuinely are, then reshape opening hours and rotas around demand — including whether 20:00 close is right on Fridays/Saturdays, and confirming the Sunday pilot staffing model.
- **Menu engineering (months 2–3):** classify every dish by popularity and margin; promote the stars, rework or retire the rest; introduce a weekly-changing specials structure that uses seasonal buying and controls waste. Target food cost: agreed % band, reviewed monthly — essential with UK food inflation still running high into 2026.
- **Labour discipline without service damage:** demand-based rotas built from booking data; cross-training so the team flexes between restaurant, bar/café, room service and events; labour as % of revenue tracked weekly. With employer NIC and wage costs where they are, this is the difference between a profitable and a loss-making year.
- **Waste and purchasing:** simple daily waste log; quarterly supplier review; portion specs on every dish.
- **Use idle capacity before buying anything:** the private dining room, the quiet 15:00–17:30 window, and Sundays are all revenue sitting in rooms we already heat and staff.

### Pillar 3 — Service and food quality: excellence as a system

The reviews tell us individual service is already warm and personal. The risk is that it depends on individuals. The fix:

- **Written service standards** for every step of the guest journey — welcome, seating, pacing (older guests should never feel rushed; a long lunch is the product, not a problem), dietary handling, bill, farewell. Short, practical, trained, audited.
- **Resident preference records:** a simple, GDPR-compliant guest book — favourite table, dietary needs, "always decaf", birthdays. For residents dining weekly, being known is the luxury.
- **Kitchen consistency:** photographed plate specs, line checks before every service, temperature and allergen controls documented (we sit inside a care-adjacent setting; food safety rigour is non-negotiable and coordinated with the wellness team).
- **Menu development with purpose:** quarterly menu refresh combining (a) resident dining committee input, (b) sales data, (c) nutrition-aware design for older adults — lighter options, high-protein dishes, diabetic-friendly desserts, smaller-portion pricing — presented at full restaurant quality, never "care catering".
- **Closing the loop:** every piece of feedback logged, themed, and answered visibly ("You said / We did" in the resident newsletter and on-site).

### Pillar 4 — Training and team development

Quality and efficiency both collapse without a developed team. The programme:

- **Induction and standards training** for every team member against the written service standards — including specific modules on serving older guests well (hearing, mobility, dietary needs, dignity, recognising changes worth flagging to the wellness team).
- **Daily 10-minute briefings** (menu, allergens, guests of note, one training point) and a weekly team review of the numbers — the team should know what good looks like and see the scoreboard.
- **Cross-training matrix:** every FOH member competent in at least two stations (restaurant, bar/barista, events); kitchen cross-cover documented.
- **Accredited development:** budget-light but real — food safety levels, allergen training, WSET Level 1–2 for front of house, first aid/dementia-friendly service awareness. Recognised qualifications aid retention in a tight labour market.
- **Career pathways:** with the Elysian–Audley merger, the group now spans 30+ villages. Benjamin's can be a place people join to grow — supervisor and duty-manager development here, progression across the group. That story helps recruitment, retention, and the group itself.
- **Recognition:** monthly service recognition linked to guest feedback, not manager favourites.

### Pillar 5 — Data analytics in decision making

Nothing exotic, and nothing new to buy — the group already owns the systems; the plan is to extract full value from them:

- **Cubigo (POS and front of house):** daily sales, product mix, voids/discounts, labour vs revenue. Resident vs external guest tagged at the point of sale so we can finally answer "how often do residents actually dine with us?"
- **Apicbase (back office):** recipe costing, allergen data, stock counts and waste tracking — the engine behind the menu-engineering and food-cost work in Pillar 2 and the allergen matrix in §8. Getting Apicbase fully and accurately populated is a named month-1–2 deliverable, because every costing decision downstream depends on it.
- **Monika (automated fridge/temperature monitoring):** HACCP logs automated rather than hand-written — staff time back, and audit-ready compliance by default.
- **Square (payments):** transaction-level data reconciled against Cubigo reporting.
- **Reservations data** (OpenTable listing we already hold): covers by daypart, no-show rate, guest origin, repeat rate.
- **Guest feedback**: 3-question QR flash survey (food, service, value) plus a quarterly resident dining survey and a standing **resident dining committee**.
- **One weekly dashboard** — a single page built from exports of the systems above. Deliberately **tool-agnostic** (data in, one page out): I understand several of these platforms are under review at director level, so the reporting layer is designed to survive any system change — and a well-instrumented Benjamin's gives directors real frontline usage evidence to feed into that review.
- **Decision rhythm:** weekly ops review against the dashboard; monthly P&L review with the GM; quarterly strategy review with senior management. Every pilot in this plan (Sunday lunch, events, hours changes, any future delivery trial) gets defined success criteria *before* launch and a stop/scale decision on the data.

**Internal tools, built in-house.** I bring hands-on AI automation engineering and application-building experience. Practically, that means I can build the connective tissue the off-the-shelf systems don't provide — at no software cost:

- Automated daily/weekly reporting that merges Cubigo, Apicbase, reservations and feedback data into the one-page dashboard without manual re-keying.
- Demand forecasting for rotas from booking and covers history.
- AI-assisted drafts for review responses, menu descriptions and resident newsletters (always human-approved before sending).
- Simple staff-facing lookups: allergen matrix, resident preferences (consent-based), daily briefing sheets generated automatically.

Anything that proves its value at Benjamin's is built to be **exportable to other Elysian–Audley sites** — documented, adaptable to local requirements, and independent of which POS or back-office platform a given site runs. This is also a direct answer to the group's positioning as a premium, innovative operator: measurable F&B management, demonstrated at one site, replicable across thirty. A detailed catalogue of these tools — scope, estimated build effort and sequencing against the 90-day plan — is set out in the separate *Automation Portfolio* document accompanying this plan.

## 5. The financial spine

The KPI baselines below are deliberately honest "to be confirmed" — but the *model* is committed now. All figures in this section are **labelled planning assumptions**, built from the published menu pricing and standard sector ratios; they will be replaced with actuals by day 30, and targets re-agreed with management at that point. The structure of the model — not the placeholder numbers — is the commitment.

### 5.1 Month-1 baseline model (illustrative, to be validated)

| Line | Planning assumption | Source / note |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly covers | ~300–400 | To be measured days 1–14 from POS |
| Average spend per head | ~£20–24 | Inferred from published menu pricing (mains £10–15, soups £8) |
| Weekly food revenue | ~£6,500–9,000 | Covers × spend/head |
| Gross margin (food) | 65–70% | Sector norm for this menu style; confirmed from invoices month 1 |
| Labour as % of revenue | TBD | Likely the key number in the business; measured week 1, band agreed with GM |
| Target by month 12 | +25–40% covers, +10% spend/head | ≈ £2,000–3,500 additional weekly revenue at validated margins |

### 5.2 Sunday lunch — break-even and success gates

Worked example (assumptions stated, validated before launch):

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Incremental labour (2 kitchen, 2–3 FOH, ~30–35 staff-hours incl. prep, fully loaded) | ~£500–600 |
| Incremental energy, consumables, marketing share | ~£100–150 |
| **Incremental Sunday cost** | **~£650–750** |
| Gross profit per cover (£30 spend/head × ~67% GM) | ~£20 |
| **Break-even** | **~33–38 covers** |

**Pre-agreed success gates (weeks 5–8 average), set before launch:**
- Covers: **≥ 45 per Sunday** (break-even plus margin for management time)
- Spend per head: **≥ £28**
- Sunday labour: **≤ 45% during the pilot**, with a route to **≤ 35% once the day is established** through higher covers and tighter prep/rota design
- Resident flash-survey satisfaction: **≥ 90% positive, no decline vs weekday baseline**
- Repeat or resident-family bookings: **≥ 20% of covers by week 8**

Decision rule: all gates met → permanent and scale marketing. Covers gate missed but trending up ≥10% week-on-week → extend four weeks. Otherwise → stop, publish the learning, redeploy the effort to afternoon tea and events. No pilot becomes permanent by default.

## 8. Risks, resident protection and compliance

| Risk | Mitigation built into the plan |
|---|---|
| External trade dilutes resident experience | Hard resident-protection rules below; external share capped; resident committee consulted before each change |
| Pilots lose money | Every pilot has pre-agreed success criteria, a fixed trial window, and a stop/scale decision — nothing becomes permanent by default |
| Cost inflation (food, labour, NIC) erodes margin | Weekly cost tracking, menu engineering, demand-based rotas, seasonal purchasing — efficiency through design, not service cuts |
| Building constraints (noise, deliveries, residential setting) | Growth weighted to daytime occasions (Sunday lunch, afternoon tea, club lunches) — naturally low-disruption |
| Food safety / dietary risk in a care-adjacent setting | Documented allergen and safety controls; standing coordination with the wellness/care team |
| Over-reach / complexity | Five pillars, one dashboard, phased rollout — anything that doesn't earn its place on the dashboard gets stopped |

### Resident-protection controls (hard rules, not aspirations)

1. **Priority booking:** residents and their guests can book any time; external bookings open a fixed window later (e.g. 14 days out) and never displace a resident request.
2. **Protected capacity:** a defined resident zone of tables at every service, and resident-only periods where demand warrants (e.g. weekday lunch core hours).
3. **External cap per service:** external bookings capped as a percentage of covers per service — set in line with the 20–25% overall share target in §6, lower at peak resident times, higher only for designated occasions (e.g. Sunday lunch) with GM agreement.
4. **Arrival process:** external guests are received via concierge/reception, signed in and shown through — preserving security and the tone of the building.
5. **Communication before change:** every pilot or change of hours is communicated to residents in advance and reviewed with the dining committee; their feedback is part of each pilot's gates.

### Compliance and governance

- **Resident data (UK GDPR):** preference records held with explicit consent, minimal data, a stated retention policy and deletion on request — coordinated with The Landsby's existing data processes.
- **Allergens and food safety:** full allergen matrix maintained in Apicbase; PPDS ("Natasha's Law") labelling on any pre-packed items (afternoon tea boxes, Landsby at Home); documented line checks, with temperature monitoring automated through Monika; coordination with the wellness team given the care-adjacent setting.
- **Safeguarding boundaries:** front-of-house staff observe and report concerns about residents (appetite changes, confusion, falls risk) to the wellness team through an agreed route — they never diagnose or intervene.
- **Licensing and building constraints:** events planned within the premises licence, noise limits and delivery-hour restrictions of a residential building — daytime-weighted growth makes this largely self-enforcing.
- **Review solicitation:** all guests invited equally to review, never incentivised or filtered — compliant with platform rules and CMA guidance on reviews.
